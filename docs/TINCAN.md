# Tin Can, Ajegunle and Mile 2 (district 12, build b58)

Twelfth district of the Lagos world plan. The mainland road that ended at the "Apapa GRA" in b57 now carries on west for another 850 m: the Tin Can Island Port wall with grain silos and a car park, a lorry bay, Ajegunle with the Boundary Roundabout, three markets, a music house, a football pitch, a digital skills centre and the local government secretariat, then Mile 2 with the Maza-Maza bridge, the interchange works and the Blue Line station, to a closed end where the Lagos-Badagry Expressway would go on.

Why west: the east end of the world is blocked by the interiors, and the world is only about 130 m wide. The rest of the plan (Bariga, Gbagada, Ikeja GRA, Alausa, Lekki) cannot attach to a single line of road without branch roads, which would be a bigger separate job.

## What is real and what is invented

Sources (public text only, no photographs were used): Wikipedia "Ajegunle", "Ajeromi-Ifelodun", "Tin Can Island Port" and "Blue Line (Lagos Metro)"; a BusinessDay report of 15 April 2026 on the Mile 2 Multi-Modal Interchange Hub and a Blueprint report on the same; a 2013 article on the closing and reopening of three Ajegunle markets.

Confirmed by those sources and used in the game (wording kept careful in the game text):

- Ajegunle is a neighbourhood in Ajeromi-Ifelodun Local Government Area, with Apapa Wharf and Tin Can to its west. The name is said to be Yoruba for "a place where riches dwell". People call it AJ City. About 550,000 residents. Since about 2008 there are more modern buildings, hotels and event centres.
- More than 70 percent of Nigeria's imported goods are said to come in through Apapa and Tin Can.
- Ajeromi-Ifelodun was set up on 1 October 1976, is about 12.5 square kilometres, mostly reclaimed marsh, had over a million people in 2022, and is called the Land of Opportunities. Ajegunle, Olodi-Apapa, Tolu, Wilmer and Boundary are among its localities. Its big markets include Boundary, Wilmer and Odunade, and Boundary Roundabout is next to Boundary Market. In 2013 three markets (Alayabiagba, Alaba, Odun-Ade) were closed over dirt and later reopened.
- Galala dance and Swo music are credited to Ajeromi-Ifelodun. Singers linked to Ajegunle include Daddy Showkey, who rose to fame in the late 1990s. Footballers linked to Ajegunle include Samson Siasia, Odion Ighalo, Taribo West, Chinwendu Ihezuo and Emmanuel Amuneke. A youth skills programme, LIFE from Paradigm Initiative, began as Ajegunle.org.
- Tin Can Island Port is in Apapa, on an island about 7 km due west of central Lagos across Lagos Harbour. Building began in 1976 and it opened in 1977. It has 12 berths with 1,358 m of quay for ships up to 260 m, and handles containers, vehicles, grain and bulk cargo. It is run by the Nigerian Ports Authority with a private terminal operator.
- Blue Line: the first electric rail line in Lagos. Phase 1 is 13 km from Lagos Marina to Mile 2 with five stations (Lagos Marina, National Theatre, Orile Iganmu, Suru Alaba, Mile 2), and opened to passengers on 4 September 2023. Phase 2, Mile 2 to Okokomaiko, began in 2023 and is expected to finish in 2027. West of Iganmu the line runs at ground level in the middle of the Lagos-Badagry Expressway, and from Iganmu to Marina it is raised.
- Mile 2 is a busy junction on the Lagos-Badagry Expressway on the road from Badagry and Agbara toward Orile, Apapa and Lagos Island. Lagos State is building a Multi-Modal Interchange Hub there (LAMATA implementing, funded by the State and the French agency AFD). Traffic on the Apapa-Oshodi Expressway there was diverted from November 2024 to about February 2026. An April 2026 report said traffic still jams beyond the newly repaired Maza-Maza Bridge.

Invented, and labelled as invented in the game:

- The layout. The order of the buildings, the compounds, the yards, the plazas, the distances, the lorry bay, the silo and crane positions, the pitch, the bridge position and the track layout at Mile 2 are not surveyed. The Boundary Roundabout is shown as a plaza with a monument, not a traffic circle.
- Every shop, stall, room and person: Mr Obi, Aunty Zainab, Mama Lateef, Mama Bose, Baba Sulaimon, Kehinde, Coach Sunday, Mr Segun, DJ Speedy, Queen Damilare, Mr Tunde, Miss Ruth, Mrs Bakare, Mr Akin, Mrs Okon, Mallam Idris, Mallam Garba, Mama Joy, the guards, the foreman, the bus and Blue Line attendants and every walker.
- The insides of all seven buildings.
- Opening hours, prices, wages, the Blue Line timetable and fare, the bus route and fare, the football and dance sessions, the school fees, the ship, the cranes, the cars and the lorries.
- The port terminal is closed to visitors. Only the outside is modelled.

## What is built

- Tin Can, north: a port wall with a shut gate (barrier, guard hut, sign), six grain silos with a gallery, three rows of imported cars, container stacks, a quay with a ship and two cranes over the water. South: the clearing agents' house, shops, and a lorry bay with 15 parked lorries.
- Ajegunle: a welcome arch over the road; the Boundary Roundabout plaza with a monument; Boundary Market hall with a stall yard behind it; Wilmer Market (walled yard with stalls); the AJ City music house; the digital skills centre; the football academy with a full pitch (lines, goals, a stand); Odunade Market (walled yard with stalls).
- Mile 2: the Maza-Maza flyover over the road with a plaza; the interchange works (hoarding, tower crane, cabins, rebar, a digger and a mixer); the local government secretariat; the Mile 2 Blue Line station with a platform, a roof, a four-car blue-and-white train, rails and a stretch of unfinished Phase 2 track; a gantry with the directions; and a closed end.
- Seven places you can walk into, each with staff, customers, an exit doorman and a closed board outside:
  - Clearing agents' house (8 AM to 6 PM): the Tin Can facts, a free sit with the agents, tea and bread.
  - Boundary Market hall (5 AM to 7 PM): foodstuff, fabrics, provisions.
  - Football academy (8 AM to 8 PM): training for a 300 kit levy (1 hour), kit room drinks.
  - AJ City music house (noon to 11 PM): a galala lesson for 1,000, watch the set (free), a bar, a DJ.
  - Digital skills centre (9 AM to 5 PM): classes at 1,200 an hour that add study hours up to Education Level 2, data and drinks.
  - Local government secretariat (8 AM to 4 PM): the Ajeromi-Ifelodun facts, a waiting hall, a canteen.
  - Mile 2 Blue Line station (7 AM to 8 PM): tickets, the Blue Line and interchange facts, snacks.
- Street people: the port gate guard, the Boundary Roundabout "AJ boy", a suya seller (from 4 PM), a Wilmer Market fruit seller, the works foreman, the Mile 2 bus attendant and a Blue Line attendant at Marina.
- Links to the rest of the world:
  - Blue Line between Mile 2 and Lagos Marina on the Island for 700 each way (45 minutes, 7 AM to 8 PM).
  - Bus between Mile 2 and Apapa Station for 500 each way (30 minutes), added to the Apapa attendant's menu.
  - Phone rides to every new place and person, and gig spots.
- Careers: clearing clerk 5,600 (Education Level 1), grain seller 3,100, sound assistant 4,300, kit assistant 3,500, centre assistant 5,600 (Education Level 2), local government clerk 6,200 (Education Level 2), Blue Line gate staff 5,000 (Education Level 1).
- Education: the digital skills centre teaches up to Education Level 2 (the school at Makoko stops at Level 1).
- Traffic: the two road lanes now run from the Island to Mile 2, about 3,150 m each, with lorries among the cars.
- Minimap: two more strips.
- World size: the playable width grew from 8,250 m to 9,260 m. The new rooms sit in two more columns past the Apapa rooms.
- The b57 closed end at the Apapa GRA is gone; the road ends at "Lagos-Badagry Expressway. Works ahead. Road closed".

## Tests (script names in the build pipeline)

- Interiors: no overlap with other rooms, every person reachable and clear of furniture, exit door reachable (seven rooms).
- Street paths: every street person and door reachable on foot; both pavements in 80 m legs along the whole road; the lorry bay, plaza, Boundary yard, Wilmer, Odunade and the works aisles, the alleys, the pitch, the flyover plazas and the station platform; a real walk along Wharf Road past the port wall; the port is shut (no path in); the road end is shut and the old Apapa end is open; the carriageway is free all the way.
- Hours, services, jobs, rides, as in b57 (125 checks); the Blue Line both ways, the bus both ways, education lessons that raise the level.
- Traffic: lights, level crossing, and the lanes carry cars both ways through every zone to Mile 2, with lorries, never overlapping and never leaving the road.
- Speed: the new views measured at all three quality tiers.
- The full regression and the older district tests (districts 3 to 11) re-run on the new build. This caught one real bug before release: the secretariat menu used the same function name as the Freedom Park gallery on Lagos Island and overwrote it. It was renamed and the gallery suite now passes in full. The only older checks that differ are the "road end is shut" ones, which are meant to be open now.

## Limits

- No photographs were used. Everything is drawn from text sources, and the layout is invented.
- The port is closed to visitors and only seen from the road; the Blue Line train is a prop at the platform (the ride is a screen fade, as the other rides are).
- The busiest view (Wilmer Market) draws about 57,000 triangles, up from about 50,000 in b57. Speed on a real phone can only be checked by the player. On the build machine it was measured with a software renderer.
