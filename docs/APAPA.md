# Costain, Ijora and Apapa (district 11, build b57)

Eleventh district of the Lagos world plan. The mainland road that ended at "Costain" in b56 now carries on west: the Costain junction under a flyover, the Ijora causeway over the lagoon, Ijora with the old thermal station and the frozen food market, and Apapa with the port wall and cranes, the truck transit park, the railway station, the club and a medical centre, to a closed end at the Apapa GRA.

## What is real and what is invented

Sources (public text only, no photographs were available): Wikipedia "Apapa", "Ijora", "Lagos Port Complex", "Apapa Container Terminal"/APM Terminals, "Tin Can Island Port", "Apapa Club" and "Oshodi-Apapa Expressway", news reports on the Eto call-up system (2021) and on the Costain flyover works (2019, Business Day), and general reports on Ijora Olopa and the Ajeloro fish market.

Confirmed by those sources and used in the game (wording kept careful in the game text):

- Apapa is a Local Government Area on the mainland, west of Lagos Island, where the lagoon meets the sea, and began as a small Awori fishing village.
- Ships could enter Lagos harbour from 1913 after moles were built at the harbour mouth; a wharf reached Apapa in 1919; four deep-water berths were finished in 1926; Apapa was chosen as the end of the western railway line.
- The Lagos Port Complex was expanded from 1956 (finished 1961) and again between 1962 and 1968. The Nigerian Ports Authority ran it until 2005, when terminals were leased to private operators. APM Terminals runs the Apapa Container Terminal. Tin Can Island Port was commissioned in 1977.
- Eto: the electronic call-up system for lorries was started on 27 February 2021 by the Nigerian Ports Authority with Truck Transit Park Limited. A lorry needs a valid call-up ticket and an electronic barrier checks it. A June 2021 report said Apapa can handle about 2,400 lorries a day but more than 7,000 arrive, and reported fake tickets and illegal toll points.
- Ijora began as a swampy village reached by canoe from Lagos Island. A coal wharf opened in 1919 and the Ijora Thermal Station was built in 1923 for the railway. The Ijora Causeway is a main road to Apapa Wharf and the Eko Bridge starts at Ijora. Ijora Olopa is a big frozen food market and the Ajeloro Fish Market is in the coal wharf area.
- Costain is a junction where Funsho Williams Avenue (Western Avenue), Apapa Road, Iganmu Road, the Eko Bridge, Abule Nla Road, Ijora Olopa and others meet, according to news reports. A new flyover replaced the old one, with part of the junction closed from 3 October to 3 December 2019.
- The Apapa GRA was laid out as a quiet residential district for colonial administrators and technical staff, on a grid of streets.
- The Apapa Club was founded in the 1930s, with tennis, swimming and squash.
- Apapa Railway Station is a Nigerian Railway Corporation terminal and the old Apapa North station is not in use.
- Dako Medical Centre, Faleti Medical Centre and the Military Hospital Lagos are in Apapa.
- The Oshodi-Apapa Expressway was built between 1975 and 1978 and serves the ports and the airport road.

Invented, and labelled as invented in the game:

- The layout. The order of the buildings, the compounds, the yards, the plaza, the causeway length, the lorry park, the crane positions and the distances are not surveyed. Distances are compressed.
- Every shop, stall, room and person: the hall warden, Mama Titus, Mama Foluke, Baba Alhaji, Mr Usman, Aunty Rashidat, Mrs Adeola, Mallam Sani, Mr Okafor, Mr Emeka, Nurse Joy, Pharmacist Ade, Mr Ezekiel, Engr. Bello, the junction warden, the gate guard, the park marshal, Aunty Sidikat, the bus attendant and every walker.
- The insides of all six buildings.
- Opening hours, prices, wages, the bus route and fare, trains, lorries, the ship and the cranes, and the amusement park (the Ferris wheel and rides are an invented touch).
- The container terminal is closed to visitors. Only the outside is modelled.

## What is built

- Costain: a plaza with trader stalls on both sides of the road and a flyover deck over it with piers, direction signs on posts, a gantry reading "Ijora. Apapa" and "Ebute Metta. Oyingbo", and a bus stop.
- The Ijora causeway: a 120 m deck over the lagoon with piers, railings, lamps and canoes on the water.
- Ijora, north: shops and the Ijora Thermal Station compound (a brick power house with a 34 m chimney, coal wharf rails, a coal heap, locomotives). South: shops and the Ijora Olopa frozen food hall, a freezer-stall yard with refrigerated lorries behind it, and the Ajeloro Fish Market water edge.
- Apapa, north: the container terminal behind a wall (its gate has a barrier and a guard and is shut to visitors), with stacks of containers, two ship-to-shore cranes and a ship at the quay; then Dako Medical Centre, an amusement park with a Ferris wheel and a carousel, and villas. South: the Truck Transit Park with about thirteen parked lorries, the call-up office, Apapa Railway Station with a platform and a train, the Apapa Club with tennis courts and a pool, and shops.
- Six places you can walk into, each with staff, customers, an exit doorman and a closed board outside:
  - Ijora Olopa frozen food hall (5 AM to 6 PM): frozen fish, chicken and turkey, dried fish and crayfish.
  - Truck call-up office (6 AM to 10 PM): the Eto facts, tea and bread, waiting with the drivers (free, 30 minutes).
  - Apapa Railway Station (5 AM to 9 PM): a ticket clerk, news and snacks, a free platform sit-down.
  - Apapa Club (10 AM to 10 PM): swimming 3,000, tennis 2,500, lunch 3,500.
  - Dako Medical Centre (8 AM to 8 PM): a check-up for 2,500, a pharmacy.
  - Ijora Thermal Station (9 AM to 4 PM): a visitor tour for 600 (45 minutes) and an engineer.
- Street people: the junction warden, the bus attendant, the port gate guard, the park marshal and a fried fish seller.
- Links to the rest of the world:
  - Bus from Ebute Metta Bus Terminal to Apapa for 400 (30 minutes), and back from the Apapa Station stop.
  - Phone rides to every new place and person, and gig spots.
- Careers: frozen food seller 3,600, call-up clerk 5,000 (Education Level 1), station porter 3,300, club waiter 3,900, clinic aide 5,800 (Education Level 2), plant operator 5,400 (Education Level 1).
- Traffic: the two road lanes now run from the Island to the Apapa GRA, about 2,300 m each, with lorries (a new vehicle model) among the cars.
- Minimap: two new strips.
- World size: the playable width grew from 7,300 m to 8,250 m. The new rooms sit in two more columns past the Ebute Metta rooms.
- The b56 closed end at Costain is gone, and the road now ends at "Apapa GRA. Road closed".

## Tests (script names in the build pipeline)

- Interiors: no overlap with other rooms, every person reachable and clear of furniture, exit door reachable (six rooms).
- Street paths: every street person and door reachable on foot; both pavements in 80 m legs along the whole road; the plaza aisles, under the flyover, the thermal station gate and coal yard, the alleys and yard aisles of the frozen food market, the truck park aisles, the station alleys and platform, the club grounds, the amusement park walks; a real walk over the causeway; the port is shut (no path into it) and its wall, stacks, gate, quay and the lorries are solid; the carriageway is free of solids every 20 m; the west end stops people and cars.
- Hours, services, jobs, rides and the bus both ways, and the old Yaba bus, as in b56.
- Traffic: the lanes carry cars both ways, there are lorries, cars stay on their road, never overlap, and the mainland lanes never cross the Makoko gate.
- Speed: the new views measured at all three quality tiers.
- Counts on this build: rooms and street paths 95 checks, traffic and signals 17, hours, services, careers and buses 103, speed measured at all three tiers (the heaviest new view drew about 50,000 triangles at the highest tier and 44,000 at the lowest, against about 146,000 for the heaviest existing view).
- The older tests were re-run on this build and all passed (Ebute Metta 99, Island 105 and 69, Makoko 46 and 83, Yaba and Oshodi 12, 37 and 94, Agege and the rail crossing 12, 35 and 81). The one check that fails is the b55 one that said the Island's west end is shut, which is no longer true by design. Then the whole existing regression suite ran with no failures.

Two small bugs were found by these runs and fixed before release:

- A walker in the new frozen-hall clinic and a club walker started on top of the door staff; a St. Jude's walker from the last build ended inside its door warden. All three routes were shortened.
- The first run of the careers check used the wrong education numbers; the game was right, the test was fixed.

## Known limits

- Everything is a model, not a survey. Real Apapa and Ijora are much larger and denser, and the real port queues are far worse.
- The container terminal cannot be entered.
- Phone speed is unmeasured. Only a real phone can say whether it stays smooth.
- You cannot drive from the mainland to the Island or to Makoko. People can walk or take the bus, the train, the coach or the ferry.
