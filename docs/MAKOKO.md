# Makoko, Lagos mainland (district 8, build b54)

Eighth district of the Lagos mainland world plan. The main road now continues west past Yaba and ends at the Makoko waterfront. Beyond the gate there are walkways, canoes and four villages on stilts over the lagoon, with the Third Mainland Bridge crossing in the distance. It was built and tested on its own before any other district is started.

Makoko is a real community where real people live and work. The game shows it as a place of work, food, school and family, and it is a respectful, invented version, not a survey.

## What is real and what is invented

Sources (text only, no photographs were available): public encyclopedia and news summaries of the Makoko-Iwaya Waterfront, the Makoko Floating School (Kunle Adeyemi, NLE), the 2012 and 2026 demolition reports, and the Egun people of Badagry.

Confirmed by those sources and used in the game:

- Makoko is on the Lagos Lagoon beside Iwaya and Yaba. Its government name is the Makoko-Iwaya Waterfront.
- The Egun people, who came from Badagry and the Republic of Benin, settled it in the nineteenth century.
- There are six villages: four on the water (Adogbo, Migbewhe, Oko Agbon, Yanshiwhe) and two on land (Apollo, Sogunro). About a third of the settlement stands on stilts.
- Fishing is the main work. Women dominate fish smoking, drying and trade. Nearly every household has a canoe and children learn to paddle from about the age of five.
- Logs are floated in across the lagoon and sawn at sawmills on the shore.
- The 2007 census did not count Makoko. Estimates of its size run from about 85,000 to over a million.
- In July 2012 demolitions left nearly 3,000 people without homes. News reports say more demolitions took place in January 2026; residents and rights groups called them unlawful and the state said it was clearing unsafe structures. The Baale character tells this plainly and says he is an invented character who cannot speak for real people.
- The Makoko Floating School was designed by Kunle Adeyemi and NLE and built in 2013 on about 250 plastic barrels. It was left empty in March 2016 and collapsed on 7 June 2016 with nobody hurt. MFS II won the Silver Lion at the 2016 Venice Architecture Biennale.
- A French-medium primary school at Oko Agbon began in 1989 with about 120 pupils.
- The Third Mainland Bridge crosses the lagoon beside Makoko.

Invented, and labelled as invented in the game:

- The layout. The game has a 260 metre slice of land road ending at a gate, a jetty, one main walkway, four cross walkways, two loop walkways and four village islands. Nothing here was surveyed.
- Every shop, stall, hall, room and person. Mama Toyin, Aunty Bose, Baba Seun, Mama Abike, Mrs Kemi, Baba Segun, Aunty Fela, the canoe men, the sawmill foreman, the net mender, the Baale and every walker are made up.
- The buildings you can enter: the fish market hall, the smokehouse, the school room, the Baale's community hall and the boat workshop. The school room is not the Floating School itself, which has no interior record I could use.
- Opening hours, prices, canoe fares, school fees and wages.
- The gate at the end of the road, the jetty and the canoe rides.
- The plank houses, fish racks and the sawmill compound.

## What is built

- About 260 m of new main road west of Yaba, ending at a gate. The Yaba end barrier is gone. A gantry reads "Iwaya Road. Makoko Waterfront".
- North of the road: shops, the smokehouse, the Baale's community hall, the boat workshop and more shops. South of the road: shops, the Makoko Fish Market (30 m wide) and more shops.
- Two land villages: Sogunro to the north and Apollo to the south, each with four rows of plank houses with zinc roofs, lanes and alleys, and name signs.
- A sawmill compound with a gate, log piles, a saw shed and a saw table, and logs floating in the water outside.
- A gate at the road end: people walk through a gap, cars cannot go past.
- The lagoon: a jetty, a main walkway 260 m long, an end pier, two loop walkways, four cross walkways, stilts under every deck, four villages on stilts with three rows of plank houses each, fish drying racks, name boards, moored canoes, fishing nets and poles in the open water, a floating school platform with an A-frame school and barrels.
- The Third Mainland Bridge beyond the last walkway: a raised deck, railings, six piers and lamps.
- The water cannot be walked on. Every one-metre square of water that is not a deck is a solid, built as 574 merged blocks.
- Five canoes paddle slowly along lanes in the open water, rocking a little.
- Five places you can walk into, each with staff, customers, an exit doorman and a closed board outside:
  - Makoko Fish Market (5 AM to 3 PM): fresh fish, crayfish and periwinkle, and dried fish counters, eight fish tables and an ice room.
  - Smokehouse (6 AM to 6 PM): smoked catfish, smoked tilapia, fish and boli, with drum smokers and a drying table.
  - Makoko Floating School (8 AM to 3 PM): cheap lessons up to Education Level 1 (500 for one hour, 1,400 for three).
  - Baale's community hall (9 AM to 5 PM): the Baale answers questions about the people, the work, the school and hard times, and you can greet him with a gift for 1,500.
  - Boat workshop (8 AM to 5 PM): paddles and model canoes, two hulls on trestles.
- Street people: a waterfront guide, two canoe men (a ride across the lagoon for 300 and a village tour for 600), a tilapia grill on the jetty, the sawmill foreman and a net mender.
- 29 walkers outside and 18 inside the rooms, all on daily schedules.
- Careers: fish smoker 3,600, fish seller 3,300, school helper 4,200 (needs Education Level 1), boat builder 4,400, sawmill hand 4,700.
- Phone: every new place and person is in the ride list and the gig spots.
- Traffic: both main-road lanes carry traffic over the new length. A bug that left older cars standing on top of each other, jamming a lane for ever, was found and fixed in this build.
- Minimap: a tenth strip for Makoko with the road, the water, decks and buildings.
- World size: the playable width grew from 4,760 m to 5,464 m. Rooms for the new interiors sit in two new columns past the Yaba rooms.

## Tests (script names in the build pipeline)

- Interiors: no overlap with other rooms, every person reachable and clear of furniture, exit door reachable (five rooms).
- Street paths: every street person and every door reachable on foot from the road, the jetty and the walkways; paths along the whole road, through the gate, along the main walkway, up and down every cross walkway and loop walkway, across the bridge to the school platform, to all four villages, along both land lanes and alleys, and through the sawmill.
- Water: nobody can stand on the water; a car cannot go past the end of the road; a person can pass the gate gap.
- Hours: each place refuses entry before opening and accepts it when open, shows or hides its closed board, and closes you out at closing time (fish market, school, boat workshop).
- Services: every price, need change and time cost, school lessons and the Level 1 stop, the Baale's facts and gift, canoe rides, lands on a deck, refusals when you are too poor, too tired or the canoes rest.
- Jobs and rides: all five shifts start and pay; the school helper is refused below Education Level 1; every new place is in the ride list; walkers follow the clock.
- Moving canoes: five canoes show near the villages, move along their lanes and hide when far away.
- Walker routes and roads: no walker route runs through a solid object; no solid blocks the main carriageway; a car drives the whole road in both lanes with traffic.
- Signals, the rail crossing and Yaba tests re-run with no violations.
- Whole existing regression suite re-run on the new build.

## Known limits

- Makoko here is a slice, not the real map. Real Makoko is much larger and denser.
- The Floating School is only a shell building with its own separate room. There is no record of its real interior.
- Cars stop at the gate. Beyond it you walk or take a canoe. The canoes you ride are a fade to the other pier, not a boat you steer.
- The moving canoes are decoration; you cannot board them directly.
- Only the five places can be entered. The plank houses and the other shops cannot be entered.
- Seated poses do not exist yet, so people in the school and hall stand or walk.
- Real photographs of Makoko were not used. If you send some, the fronts and the layout can be corrected from them.
