# Iddo, Ebute Metta and Oyingbo (district 10, build b56)

Tenth district of the Lagos world plan. The Island's closed west end is now the Carter Bridge, so you can walk from Lagos Island to the mainland. On the far side is Iddo with the old Lagos Terminus and a bus terminal, then Ebute Metta with the Railway Corporation, Mobolaji Johnson Station, St. Jude's, Lisabi Hall and a school and hospital front, and at the west end Oyingbo Market with its stall yard and the Red Line overpass. It was built and tested on its own before any other district is started.

## What is real and what is invented

Sources (text only, no photographs were available): Wikipedia "Ebute Metta", Wikipedia "Carter Bridge", Wikipedia "Lagos Terminus", Wikipedia "Lagos-Ibadan railway", the Guardian Nigeria report on Oyingbo Market (15 March 2015), and a November 2023 news report on the Oyingbo overpass for the Lagos Red Line.

Confirmed by those sources and used in the game:

- Ebute Metta means "the three harbours", for Iddo, Otto and Oyingbo, and it belongs to the old Awori kingdom of Otto.
- In 1867 Egba Christians who had come to Lagos settled there on land granted with the help of Governor John Hawley Glover. They called it Ago Egba and built St. Jude's Church and schools.
- Railways from 1914 made Ebute Metta a place for people from the interior. Lisabi Hall was built by the Ago Egba in 1938. Many third-generation descendants became engineers, technicians and stationmasters for the Nigerian Railway Corporation, which has its headquarters there.
- Real places named in the area: the post office, St. Paul's Catholic Church, St. Saviour's School, Ajayi Memorial Hospital, Oyingbo Market and a big bus terminal.
- The Carter Bridge was built by the British colonial government and opened in 1901, named after Sir Gilbert Thomas Carter. It joins Lagos Island (ending at Idumota) to the mainland (starting at Iddo), was the only bridge link for a long time, was rebuilt in the late 1970s, and its Iddo end has the Alaka-Ijora flyover (1973). Its streetlights were commissioned in July 2013.
- The Lagos Terminus at Iddo stands near the lagoon on Iddo Island. Nigeria's first railway line began in Lagos in 1898. The old line is narrow gauge (1,067 mm), and the terminus was the main station until 2021.
- The Lagos to Ibadan standard-gauge line (157 km) was commissioned on 10 June 2021 at Ebute Metta Station, now Mobolaji Johnson Station.
- Oyingbo Market was established in the early 1920s and is described as one of the oldest and busiest markets in Lagos. The original was pulled down and a new market commissioned later.
- The Oyingbo overpass for the Red Line was commissioned by Governor Sanwo-Olu (November 2023 report), with a bus park. It rises from Murtala Mohammed Way and comes down onto the Iddo and Costain roads, and is the second of five bridges on the rail corridor.

Invented, and labelled as invented in the game:

- The layout. The road, the order of the buildings, the compounds, the stall yard, the platforms and the bridge length are not surveyed. Distances are compressed, and the bridge is a 140 m model.
- Every shop, stall, hall, room and person: Mama Putu, Mama Folashade, Baba Idowu, Mr Adebayo, Aunty Bisi, Mrs Okonkwo, Mallam Ibrahim, Mr Okeke, Miss Hadiza, the sexton, the vicar, Mr Taiwo, Mrs Bakare, Aunty Kemi, Baba Sule, the bridge warden, the rail watchman, the bus attendant and every walker.
- The insides of all six buildings.
- Opening hours, prices, wages, the bus route and fare, the train route and fare, and the times.
- The old train, the modern train and the buses.

## What is built

- The Island road now continues west across the Carter Bridge: a deck over the lagoon with piers, railings, lamps and a gantry reading "Carter Bridge. Opened 1901", then on for about 620 m of mainland road to a closed west end at "Costain".
- Iddo, north side: shops, and the Lagos Terminus compound (a long station building with a colonnade and clock tower, a platform, an old narrow-gauge train, rails). South side: the bus terminal compound with shelters and parked danfos.
- Ebute Metta, north side: shops, the Post Office, the Nigerian Railway Corporation tower, St. Jude's compound (tower, nave, lawn, gate), Lisabi Hall. South side: shops, Mobolaji Johnson Station with a platform and a modern train, St. Paul's compound (outside only), a school, a hospital, more shops.
- Oyingbo: a market hall on the road and a yard behind it of about 100 stalls in five rows, and an overpass over the road with a rail line, then more shops to the closed end.
- Six places you can walk into, each with staff, customers, an exit doorman and a closed board outside:
  - Oyingbo Market hall (5 AM to 6 PM): provisions, fruit and vegetables, household goods.
  - Lagos Terminus (5 AM to 8 PM): a clerk, a news and snacks counter, a free platform-watching sit-down.
  - Mobolaji Johnson Station (5 AM to 9 PM): a ticket clerk and a refreshments counter.
  - Nigerian Railway Corporation (8 AM to 4 PM): reception and records. No real records are held.
  - St. Jude's Church (7 AM to 6 PM): a sexton, a vicar, quiet prayer (free, 15 minutes), a candle for 200, and the facts.
  - Lisabi Hall (10 AM to 8 PM): a gathering for 500 (two hours, raises fun and social).
- Street people: the bridge warden, the bus attendant, an akara seller (5 AM to noon), a roasted plantain seller (11 AM to 10 PM) and the rail watchman.
- Links to the rest of the world:
  - Bus from the Ebute Metta Bus Terminal to Yaba Terminal for 300 (30 minutes), and "Coach to Ebute Metta" at Yaba Terminal for 300.
  - Train from Mobolaji Johnson Station to Agege for 400 (30 minutes), and "Ticket to Ebute Metta" at Agege Station for 400.
  - Phone rides to every new place and person, and gig spots.
- Careers: market seller 3,400, railway clerk 5,200 (needs Education Level 1), station hand 4,100, sexton 3,200.
- Minimap: two new strips for the bridge and Ebute Metta.
- World size: the playable width grew from 6,330 m to 7,300 m. The new rooms sit in two more columns past the Island rooms.
- Traffic: the two Island lanes now run over the bridge to Oyingbo, 1,480 m each, and carry cars both ways. Cars still cannot reach the Island from the mainland roads. The Makoko gate stops them. The bridge, the bus and the train are the ways across.

## Corrections made this build

- Yaba text: the in-game "About Yaba station" and the Yaba notes said the standard-gauge station was in Yaba. It is at Ebute Metta, so the game text and the Yaba notes now say so.

## Tests (script names in the build pipeline)

- Interiors: no overlap with other rooms, every person reachable and clear of furniture, exit door reachable (six rooms).
- Street paths: every street person and door reachable on foot from the pavement near it; whole pavements on both sides of the road in 80 m legs from the Island end to the closed end; the gate and platforms at the terminus, round both sides of the terminus building, the bus terminal bays, the alley to the station platform and along the train, both church compounds, the Oyingbo alley and all four yard aisles, and under the overpass.
- The bridge: a real walk, driven by the same walking code a player's tap uses, from the Island end over the Carter Bridge to Iddo; the railings and the lagoon are solid; the carriageway is free of any solid every 20 m from the Island to the closed end; the closed end stops people and cars.
- Hours: each place refuses entry before opening and accepts it when open, shows or hides its closed board, and closes you out at closing time.
- Services: every price, need change and time cost; the prayer, candle, gathering, platform sit-down; the sourced facts in every dialog; refusals when too poor or when the seller is not out.
- Jobs and rides: all four shifts start and pay; the Education Level 1 gate; every new place is in the ride list; walkers follow the clock.
- Bus and train: both directions of each, price, time cost, landing on a walkable spot, refusals when too poor, rest at night, and the Yaba and Agege menus still sell their other tickets. The Yaba station fact is corrected.
- Traffic: the Island lanes carry cars both ways over the bridge and along Ebute Metta, cars stay on that road, never overlap, and the mainland lanes never cross the Makoko gate.
- The older tests were re-run on this build with no new failures: the Island services and walks (b55), Makoko and Yaba (b54, b53), Agege, Oshodi and the rail crossing (b52), and then the whole existing regression suite. The only b55 check that now fails is the one that said the Island's west end is shut, which is no longer true by design.

Two bugs were found by these runs and fixed before release:

- Five of the six new buildings had the wrong exit link, so leaving put the player nowhere. Found by the hours test, fixed.
- A car that restarts at the east end of the Island road could briefly appear over the harbour water. The lane start was moved west.

## Known limits

- The bridge, the road and every building are models, not a survey. Real Ebute Metta, Iddo and Oyingbo are larger and denser.
- You cannot drive from the mainland to the Island or to Makoko. People can walk or take the bus, the train, the coach or the ferry.
- St. Paul's, the school, the hospital and the shops are outside only.
- The trains are standing models, not moving trains. The old train and the modern train do not run.
- Seated poses do not exist yet, so people in the church, the station and the hall stand or walk.
- Real photographs were not used. If you send some, the fronts and the layout can be corrected from them.
