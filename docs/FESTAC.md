# Festac Town, the Trade Fair, Alaba and Okokomaiko (district 13, build b59)

Thirteenth district of the Lagos world plan. The road that ended at the Mile 2 "Works ahead" sign in b58 now carries on west for another 900 m along the Lagos-Badagry Expressway: the gated Festac Town estate with its three gates, a mall with a cinema, the Lagos International Trade Fair Complex, Agboju market, the Alaba electronics market, Okokomaiko with its market and bus park, and the Blue Line Phase 2 station site, to a closed end where the expressway would go on.

Why this district: the plan's remaining districts (Bariga, Gbagada, Ikeja GRA, Alausa, Lekki) cannot attach to a single line of road without branch roads, which is a bigger separate job. The next real places along the same expressway, past Mile 2, are Festac, the Trade Fair and Alaba, so they fit without changing how the map works.

## What is real and what is invented

Sources (public text only, no photographs were used): Wikipedia "Festac Town", "Satellite Town, Lagos", "Lagos Trade Fair Complex", "Alaba International Market" and "Blue Line (Lagos Transit)"; a Wikivoyage mirror for Festac (markets, the mall and cinema, danfo fares); an ICIR report of 11 March 2020 on the roads to Alaba.

Confirmed by those sources and used in the game (wording kept careful in the game text):

- Festac Town is a federal housing estate in Amuwo-Odofin Local Government Area, built to house visitors to FESTAC 77, the Second World Black and African Festival of Arts and Culture held in 1977. It has 5,000 dwelling units and seven major avenues (1st to 7th), and was designed for up to 45,000 visitors. After the festival the Federal Government allocated the homes by ballot. Three main gates (First, Second, Third) open from the Lagos-Badagry Expressway into the 1st, 2nd and 7th Avenues. Minor roads branch from the avenues, and the cul-de-sacs (closes) are named alphabetically. The 6th Avenue is reached by a bridge from the 1st Avenue. Recently it has gained banks, shopping complexes, hotels and nightlife; who manages it is unclear, since the federal, state and local governments all claim it.
- A travel guide lists a large Festac mall with cinemas (open 10 AM to 10 PM, tickets about 800 to 2,500 naira) and the markets Ahonsi (the 23rd market, called the biggest), Agboju (2nd Avenue) and Oyinlola Ifelodun. It says bargaining is expected and designer goods in markets are likely fake. The same guide gives danfo fares of about 100 to 1,000 naira.
- Satellite Town lies along the expressway in Amuwo-Odofin and was set up by Lagos State in early 1960 to help low-income earners own homes.
- The Lagos International Trade Fair Complex opened in 1977, covers 350 hectares in Satellite Town, has twelve exhibition halls and a motel complex of 100 chalets, and belongs to the Federal Government. Jewellery, auto spare parts and consumer goods traders moved there from Balogun Market in 2002. Its infrastructure has been poorly maintained. (Designed by Zoran Bojovic, built by Energoprojekt.)
- Alaba International Market is in Ojo and is called the largest electronics market in Nigeria; traders sell electronics and repair home appliances. In June 2023 officials announced structurally defective buildings would be demolished; the Lagos State Building Control Agency named 17 buildings as beyond redemption. A 2020 report said the roads to it (Alaba, Aka, Okoko and others) were full of craters, and people it interviewed said a 20-minute drive could take an hour and a half. Okokomaiko, Ajangbadi and Sabo are nearby communities.
- Blue Line Phase 2 runs from Mile 2 to Okokomaiko; construction began in 2023 and was expected to finish in 2027; it will connect with the future Purple Line. The whole line is 27 km with 13 stations. The sources do not name the Phase 2 stations, so the game does not either.

Invented, and labelled as invented in the game:

- The layout. The order of the buildings, the estate's street plan and block shapes, the compound layouts, the arch, the gantry and the position of the station site are not surveyed. Festac is drawn at a tiny scale next to the road, not as the real grid.
- Every shop, stall, room and person: Mrs Adaeze, Mama Nkem, Miss Lola, Mr Dami, Mrs Eke, Mr Okey, Mrs Fola, Miss Rita, Mr Jide, Mama Esther, Mama Toyin, Mr Naz, Mr Chidi, Mr Emeka, Mr Ladi, Mama Ngozi, Bayo, the guards, the conductor and every walker.
- The insides of all seven buildings.
- Opening hours, prices, wages, the cinema film, the motel rates, the bus route and fare (600 each way, 45 minutes).
- The Okokomaiko Phase 2 station site is a construction site only: no trains run, and the game says so.

## What is built

- Festac Town, north: the estate office, a long wall with three gates (First, Second, Third) and an avenue behind each one, about forty three-storey blocks, cross streets, guard huts and trees.
- Festac, south: the Festival-style mall with a car park; the Trade Fair auto parts hall and the Trade Fair Motel; the Trade Fair compound with a gate, four exhibition halls, ten chalets and a fountain.
- Agboju market hall and the Alaba electronics plaza, with the walled Alaba market yard (stalls in three rows) behind a gate.
- An arch over the road ("Alaba, Ojo, Okokomaiko"), shops all along both sides, a gantry with the directions, the Okokomaiko market yard, and the Okokomaiko bus park with its office and parked danfos.
- The Phase 2 station site at the west end: a hoarding, a platform frame on columns, a tower crane, cabins, rebar, rails ending in a buffer. The Phase 2 rails run on from the Mile 2 platform along the back of the south side to the site, with signs saying "track works".
- A closed end at the west: "Lagos-Badagry Expressway. Works ahead. Road closed".
- Seven places you can walk into, each with staff, customers, an exit doorman and a closed board outside:
  - Festac Town Estate Office (8 AM to 4 PM): the Festac facts, a free sit in the lounge, tea and snacks.
  - Festival Mall (10 AM to 10 PM): a cinema ticket for 1,500 (2 hours, a big boost to fun), popcorn and drinks, a supermarket till.
  - Trade Fair Auto Parts Hall (8 AM to 6 PM): the Trade Fair and Satellite Town facts, a walk through the hall, a jewellery counter.
  - Trade Fair Motel (open all day and night): a chalet for 8 hours (6,500), a day chalet, a shower, breakfast.
  - Agboju Market (5 AM to 7 PM): fish and meat, fried food, household goods.
  - Alaba Electronics Plaza (8 AM to 7 PM): phone accessories, the Alaba market and Ojo roads facts, a look around.
  - Okokomaiko Bus Park Office (5 AM to 9 PM): the bus to Mile 2, the Phase 2 and transport facts, snacks.
- Street people: the First Gate guard, the Trade Fair gate guard, a phone-case hawker at Alaba and the bus conductor at Okokomaiko.
- Links to the rest of the world:
  - Bus between Okokomaiko and Mile 2 for 600 each way (45 minutes, 5 AM to 9 PM), also on the Mile 2 bus attendant's menu.
  - Phone rides to every new place and person, and gig spots.
- Careers: estate clerk 6,000 (Education Level 2), cinema usher 3,600, parts clerk 4,800, motel porter 4,200, fish seller 3,000, electronics assistant 5,200 (Education Level 1), park clerk 3,800.
- Traffic: the two road lanes now run from the Island to Okokomaiko, about 4,050 m each, with lorries among the cars.
- Minimap: two more strips.
- World size: the playable width grew from 9,260 m to 10,290 m. The new rooms sit in two more columns past the Mile 2 rooms.
- The b58 closed end at Mile 2 is gone.

## Tests (script names in the build pipeline)

- New-area suites on the final build: 17 checks (zones, road bounds, traffic), 93 checks (interiors, street paths, a real scripted walk) and 114 checks (hours, services, jobs, rides). All pass.
- Traffic: lights, the level crossing, and the lanes carry cars both ways through every zone to Okokomaiko, with lorries, never overlapping and never leaving the road.
- Interiors: no overlap with other rooms, every person reachable and clear of furniture, exit door reachable (seven rooms).
- Street paths: every street person and door reachable on foot; both pavements in 80 m legs along the whole road; all three estate avenues and cross streets, the alleys, the mall car park, the Trade Fair gate and aisles, the Alaba and Okokomaiko yards, the bus park, the station site, and a real walk along the road past the estate wall; the road end is shut and the old Mile 2 end is open; the carriageway is free all the way. 633 walkers checked, none inside a solid.
- Hours, services, jobs, rides: opening and closing times, closing time ejection, every service, every fact, the cinema, the motel night, both bus directions, too poor and night refusals, the seven careers, the ride list, walkers following the clock.
- Name clashes: every one of the 351 existing people, 63 jobs and 76 interiors from the previous build was compared with this build and is unchanged. This check came from a real bug found in testing: two new job names (cinema usher, fish seller) first reused existing names and overwrote the Alausa cinema usher and Ijora frozen-fish jobs. They are now fzusher and fishseller.
- Speed: the new views measured at all three quality tiers. The busiest view is about 58,000 to 62,000 triangles at the high tier (the previous build's busiest was about 57,000); at the low tier it is about 44,000.
- The older district test suites (Island, Apapa, Ijora, Tin Can) were re-run on this build. The only differences are old checks that said "the road ends at Mile 2" or "lanes stop at the old bound"; the road now continues, so those checks are obsolete. The "both ends of the Island road shut" check was already obsolete in the previous build.
- A random street-mugger event can interrupt a scripted walk with a dialog; the walk test switches it off. This is a test-only measure, not a game change.

## Limits

- No photographs were used. Everything is drawn from text sources, and the layout is invented.
- Festac is a small stand-in for the estate: three gates, three avenues and a few dozen blocks, not the real grid. No Phase 2 trains run, and the station site is closed to visitors.
- Speed on a real phone can only be checked by the player.
