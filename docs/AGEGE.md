# Agege, Lagos mainland (district 6, build b52)

Sixth district of the Lagos mainland world plan. The main road now continues west past Ikeja into Agege, a railway town: a level crossing with a moving commuter train, Agege Station, a bakery for the famous Agege bread, Alade market, a stadium with a gym, a canteen, an Oba statue plaza, and Orile Agege, the old town street. It was built and tested on its own before any other district is started.

## What is real and what is invented

Sources (text only, no photographs were available): Wikipedia "Agege", the Guardian (Lagos) article "Agege and the Greater Lagos train" (the Pen Cinema bridge opening), the Lagos State Public Procurement Agency page on the Pen Cinema flyover, The Ikeja Record on traders under the Pen Cinema bridge, and the Wikivoyage Agege guide.

Confirmed by those sources and used in the game:

- Agege is a large area of Lagos State, once the capital of Ikeja Division until 1927. The name is believed to come from Aige (or Ayige), a hunter and warrior from Otta who settled the land. Another account says Hausa settlers named it after the tree cutters hired to clear it.
- "Orile Agege" is the old settlement. "Agege Station" is a trading town that grew beside the railway in the late nineteenth century. By 1912 it had about 4,000 to 5,000 settled people, about 60 percent Hausa.
- The railway linked Lagos with Abeokuta, then Ibadan, then the north. Cocoa was collected, sorted and graded at Agege for export. About 70 percent of the kola nuts sent to the Northern Province passed through the rail-head, according to a mid-twentieth-century claim quoted on Wikipedia.
- Jacob Coker founded the Agege Planters' Union in 1907, described as the first agricultural cooperative in West Africa.
- Agege bread is unsliced, soft, and keeps well. It was unnamed until locals called it Agege bread as its popularity grew.
- Alade market and the Abattoir meat market are major markets there.
- Orile Agege is known for the Egungun and Gelede festivals. It has a Baale tradition and three recognised Obas in the area. There is an Oba Agege statue.
- Agege Stadium is in Ijaiye, a multipurpose venue used for local and international games, with a gym. Agege Train Station (Babatunde Fashola Station) is at 40 Olugbosho Street and runs from 7 AM to 8:30 PM.
- The Pen Cinema flyover is about 1.4 km long and carries the road over the railway corridor at the Pen Cinema intersection. It was opened in March 2021. Traders used to sell under the bridge and on the tracks.

Invented, and labelled as a guess:

- The layout. Real Agege is many square kilometres with the railway on one side of the main road. The game has a 560 metre slice with the railway crossing the road level, with gates, and a side street for the old town.
- The Pen Cinema flyover is not built as a drivable flyover. The game has a level crossing with gates, and the Pen Cinema is only a landmark building with its name on it. The crossing watchman says so.
- Every shop, bakery, canteen, market stall and person name. Mama Bolu, Mama Toyin, Mallam Hamza, Aunty Sidi, Coach Seun, Mr Emeka are made up. The building for the Planters' Union, the plaza, the stadium layout, the compounds and the old town houses are all invented.
- Opening hours except the station (7 AM to 8:30 PM, from Wikivoyage). Prices, the train fare to Oshodi, and the gym prices.
- The train. It is a green and white commuter train of a loco and four coaches. Real train frequency and livery were not sourced. The game runs one about every minute or two while the station is open.
- No Egungun mask or ritual is shown. The dancer is a person in bright cloth who talks about the festival and dances in the evening. The game does not show anything sacred.

## What is built

- About 560 m of new main road west of Ikeja, ending at a road-works barrier. The old Ikeja end barrier is gone.
- The level crossing: a single railway line running north and south across the road, with ballast, sleepers and rails, crossing planks, masts with flashing red lamps, and two gate arms that lower and raise. A commuter train of a loco and four coaches runs through every minute or two between 7 AM and 8:30 PM, in either direction, and none at night. Traffic stops at the gates and flows again. If you stand on the line the train pushes you clear, and the same for your car.
- Agege Station: the building on the main road, a platform beside the line with a shelter, bench and signs, and the ticket hall inside.
- The Pen Cinema landmark building and a bus stop.
- North of the road: shops, the bakery, shops west of the line, Mama Bolu's buka and a long row of shops to the old town.
- South of the road: shops, the Oba statue plaza, the Planters' Union hall, Alade market hall with a stall yard, the station bus park with parked danfos and okadas, the stadium (pitch with markings, goals, stands, floodlights, a gated front and a fence) with a gym building, shops, and walled old compounds.
- Orile Agege: a street running north from the main road with a gantry sign, walled earth-coloured compounds on both sides and trees.
- Five places you can walk into, each with staff, customers, an exit doorman and a closed board outside:
  - Agege Station (7 AM to 8:30 PM): ticket hall with a counter, benches, a departures board and a platform gate. Buy a ticket to Oshodi for 300: the train takes you to the Oshodi interchange and the trip takes half an hour.
  - Agege Bread Bakery (5 AM to 9 PM): a brick oven, loaves, a dough table. Agege bread, bread and akara, bread and tea, bread and egg, sugar bread. The baker tells how the bread got its name.
  - Alade Market (6 AM to 7 PM): two rows of stalls, a foodstuff counter, a meat section and a fabric counter. Roast plantain, beans, oranges, garri, a bag of rice, grilled beef, asun, ponmo, Ankara, lace and a head tie.
  - Agege Stadium Gym (7 AM to 9 PM): treadmills, a weights rack, a boxing ring, a shake bar. Sessions cost 1,500, 2,500 and 3,500 and raise your fitness, which makes you run a little faster. This uses the same fitness number as the other gym in the game.
  - Mama Bolu Buka (8 AM to 10 PM): a hot counter of pots, tables with plastic chairs. Amala with ewedu and gbegiri, eba and egusi, pounded yam and efo, jollof and fish, pepper soup.
- Street people: a crossing watchman, a bread hawker, a kola seller, Mallam Suya (5 PM to 4 AM), the elder of the Planters' Union, a tour guide on the plaza, the stadium gate marshal, the chief of Orile Agege and an Egungun dancer who dances in the evening. Each tells the researched facts above.
- About 24 walkers on the streets, platform, plaza, market yard, bus park, stadium pitch and the old town street, and 16 inside the rooms, all on daily schedules.
- Careers: station ticket clerk 4,600 (needs Education Level 1), gym attendant 4,200, bakery assistant 3,800, buka cook 3,600, market seller 3,000 per shift base (same shift system and education pay bonus as before).
- Phone: every new place and street person is in the ride list and the gig spots.
- Traffic: both main-road lanes run the full length to the new end, with new cars, danfos and okadas. The earlier vehicles were shifted so they keep their places.
- Minimap: an eighth strip for Agege with the road, the railway, Orile street, the stadium pitch and the buildings.
- World size: the playable width grew from 3,360 m to 4,070 m. Rooms for the new interiors sit in two new columns past the Ikeja rooms.

## Tests (script names in the build pipeline)

- Rail crossing: 800 seconds simulated, at least nine trains, no vehicle enters the crossing once the gates are closing, no vehicle is ever inside the train, traffic stops at the gates and flows again, gates are closed about 11 seconds at a time and the crossing is open most of the time, gate arms rise again, no trains at night, the train pushes a player off the line.
- Signals: 300 seconds simulated, zero vehicles cross a stop line on red at Ojuelegba, Agege or Allen, and cars queue and flow at all twelve approaches. A car already past the line when the light changes may finish crossing.
- Interiors: no overlap with other rooms, every person reachable, door reachable, never walking through furniture; street paths to every outside person from the main road and from Orile street; paths along the whole new road, up Orile street, along the platform, across the level crossing and onto the stadium pitch.
- Hours: each place refuses entry before opening and shows the board, accepts entry when open, and closes you out at closing time including the station's half-hour close.
- Services: bread, akara, beans, grilled beef, fabric, gym sessions, amala, hawker bread, kola, suya, the train ticket. Every price, need change and time cost is checked. A buyer without cash is refused and nothing moves.
- Jobs and rides: the five shifts start and pay; the ticket clerk job is refused below Education Level 1; every new place is in the ride list; walkers follow the clock.
- Walker routes and the road: no walker route runs through a solid object, and no solid blocks the main carriageway, Orile street or the track.
- Whole existing regression suite re-run on the new build.

## Known limits

- Agege here is a slice about 560 m long. The real district is many square kilometres.
- The Pen Cinema flyover is not built; the road crosses the railway at ground level with gates.
- The train does not stop at the platform, and the ticket is one-way. The way back from Oshodi is a ride or a drive.
- Only the main road carries traffic. Orile street has none.
- Only the five places can be entered. The other shops share a few fronts.
- Parked cars and danfos are static.
- Seated poses do not exist yet, so people in the canteen and the station stand or walk.
- The stadium stands are plain blocks and nobody plays on the pitch. Joggers run on it in the early morning and the evening.
- Real photographs of Agege were not used. If you send some, the fronts and the layout can be corrected from them.
